# Geometry-led choreography

Use this guide to discover motion from a logo rather than placing generic
effects on top of it.

## Read the mark as a system

Look for:

- **modules:** repeated dots, bars, tiles, rings, leaves, or letters;
- **relationships:** containment, overlap, offset, alignment, or a fixed ratio;
- **paths:** diagonals, curves, baselines, corners, or implied trajectories;
- **negative space:** gaps, cuts, holes, and masks that can reveal or hide;
- **symmetry:** rotational, mirrored, radial, or deliberately asymmetric;
- **visual mass:** which part feels heavy, anchored, or secondary;
- **material states:** filled, outlined, clear, tinted, monochrome, light, dark;
- **optical center:** where the mark appears centered, which may differ from its
  mathematical bounding box.

Turn each observation into an affordance. A detached dot can orbit. A carved
corner can become a moving aperture. Parallel stripes can chase or wipe along
their shared axis. Nested shapes can compress, trade scale, or unfold.

## Concept families

These are ingredients, not presets.

### Assembly

Parts enter along their own axes and lock into the resting mark. Use when the
logo is visibly modular. The reverse action can become the exit or loop seam.

### Compression and release

One part yields while another gains mass. Use an existing size relationship so
the deformation reads as energy transfer rather than arbitrary squash.

### Size trade

Two related parts exchange scale or hierarchy. Preserve their ratio at the
handoff and make the negative space participate.

### Orbit or corner circuit

A detached part travels around an anchor, through meaningful corners, or along
an implied boundary. Choose the path from the logo, not from a generic circle.

### Axis sweep

Parallel bars, diagonals, or a cut edge reveal the next state through a
directional mask. Align the wipe to the mark's actual angle.

### Aperture or carve

Animate a mask, hole, or negative-space element to reveal material beneath it.
The void becomes an active part of the identity.

### Material journey

Cycle authoritative light, dark, monochrome, tinted, or glass variants while a
continuous movement hides the raster or material handoff.

### Dimensional turn

Use an edge-on flip when two faces or material states imply front/back. Match
the outgoing and incoming rotations at the edge so the object keeps one
identity.

### Spinner derivation

Find the smallest recognizable subset of the logo and animate that subset as a
loader. The full logo cycle can then expand the same grammar into a longer
sequence.

### Line or path travel

Use stroke drawing, segment chase, or path-following only when lines are native
to the logo. Preserve stroke weight at small sizes.

## Choreography principles

### One object, continuous energy

When a visual state changes at peak motion, the incoming state inherits the
outgoing state's position, angle, scale, and velocity. A reset at the handoff
breaks the illusion.

### Anticipation must pay off

A wind-up should predict the direction or force of the primary move. Do not add
a pre-bounce merely to make the mark feel animated.

### Follow-through belongs to secondary mass

Let a smaller or lighter part finish slightly after the primary body. This adds
detail without making the entire logo bounce.

### Stillness gives motion value

An always-moving identity becomes a spinner, even when nothing is loading.
Ambient brand cycles need a deliberate dwell; loaders need a state-based reason
to continue.

### Overlap related beats

Camera pullback, compression, rotation, and part travel can overlap when they
support one action. Avoid independent easing resets that make the cycle feel
like several demos stitched together.

### Use the logo to hide the trick

Good handoff moments include:

- edge-on rotation;
- maximum blur during fast movement;
- complete mask occlusion;
- overlap behind another part;
- a moment where both variants share the same silhouette.

## Motion vocabulary for specs

Use precise terms:

- **anticipation:** a preparatory move opposite or smaller than the main action;
- **handoff:** the exact moment identity, layer, or material changes;
- **continuity transition:** before and after remain legibly the same object;
- **morph:** geometry interpolates rather than merely crossfading;
- **crossfade:** outgoing and incoming opacity overlap in the same position;
- **follow-through:** secondary motion resolves after the primary mass;
- **asymmetric easing:** acceleration and deceleration use different rates;
- **perceptual duration:** the point where a spring appears settled;
- **loop seam:** the boundary between the final and first frames;
- **optical pivot:** the visually correct center of rotation.

## Concept quality test

A strong concept answers yes to most of these:

- Would this motion still make sense if color and blur were removed?
- Can its main idea be traced to a specific logo feature?
- Is the resting logo exact and recognizable?
- Does the loader form feel related to the full cycle?
- Can the transition be understood at the smallest target size?
- Does the loop have one dominant idea rather than several unrelated tricks?
- Is every secondary effect supporting the dominant idea?
